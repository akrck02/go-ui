📐 Layout

Every control is a box, and boxes nest. Layout happens in End, once the whole tree is known, so a box can grow into space that depends on boxes declared after it.

Asking for a layout

A box’s Layout says:

Field Meaning
Direction Horizontal (children left to right, the default) or Vertical (top to bottom)
Width, Height Fit() (as big as the content, the default), Grow() / GrowBy(weight) (the content plus a share of the free space), Share(weight) (a share of the free space, starting from nothing: equal shares come out equal, so table columns line up), Fixed(pixels), Percent(share); .Between(min, max) bounds any of them
Padding Space inside the edges: Pad(all), PadXY(horizontal, vertical) or each side
Gap Space between children
AlignX, AlignY Start, Center or End: where children sit when there is room to spare
Columns Above 0, a grid: children fill rows of this many equal cells, with Gap both ways
Wrap On a horizontal box, children that would pass the right edge go on to a new line, like words (a row of tags); give the box a width that does not follow its content (Grow in a column, Fixed) 
interfaceContext.Box(ui.Layout{Direction: ui.Vertical, Width: ui.Grow(), Gap: 8}, func() { … })
interfaceContext.Frame(layout, ui.Look{Fill: theme.Surface, Border: theme.Line, BorderWidth: 1, Radius: 6}, func() { … })

Box only arranges; Frame also paints a fill, a border, rounded corners, a shadow or a clip (Look). Because layouts and looks are plain structs, they can come from a file one day, like a theme.

How sizes are worked out

The work is done one axis at a time:

  1. Fit, from the leaves up: a text is as wide as it measures, an icon as its size; a box adds up its children along its direction (plus gaps) or takes the biggest across it, plus padding.
  2. Grow, from the root down: percentages take their share of the parent; along the direction, growing children split the space left in proportion to their weights; across it, they take the parent’s size. When the content does not fit (a narrow window), the growing children shrink instead, each giving up part of what it has above its minimum, so fixed and fitted boxes around them (a side column, an inspector, a button) keep their size.
  3. Place, from the root down, with padding, gaps and alignment.

Text does not wrap yet, so heights never depend on widths, except in a box with Wrap: its height is worked out once the widths are known, which the axis-by-axis order allows. Content that still does not fit overflows at the end and is cut by the panel. A line of text in a box whose look clips (Look{Clip: true}, like a table cell) ends in “…” where it would pass the box’s edge.

A whole application

The same boxes lay out a whole window. Here the register (cmd/app) is a column of a menu bar, a toolbar and a row; the row holds a fixed navigation list, a growing middle (cards in Share columns over a table) and a fixed inspector. Narrow the window and only the middle shrinks.

Shortcuts for the common cases (Row, Column, Spacer, Separator, Grid) are in the controls.
