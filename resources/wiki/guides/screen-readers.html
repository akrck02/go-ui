🗣️ Screen readers

A screen reader speaks what is on the screen for a person who cannot see it, and lets them act on it: on a phone, TalkBack reads what a finger moves over, and a double tap presses it. It needs the screen as things with names, not pixels. While one is on (SetScreenReader(true), which the window does on Android), every frame also makes a list of what can be read, worked out from the boxes themselves, at no cost while none is on.

How the boxes are read

  • A control is a node, read by the texts inside it: a button reads “Light the lamp, button”. A control inside another (a switch in a row) is a node of its own, inside the row.
  • Texts beside each other in one box are read together: a row’s title and the line under it are one node, and a paragraph is one, not a node per word.
  • A scroll area holds its rows, and only those that show are read; the screen reader can scroll it on (ReaderForward) or back.
  • What is behind a modal or a sheet is left out, as it cannot be touched.
  • The node the screen reader is on is ringed (SetScreenReaderFocus), dark outside and light inside, over everything, as a phone rings what TalkBack reads.

Describing a control of your own

The controls say what they are and their state: a checkbox ticked, a toggle on (a switch), the tab shown, a slider’s range, a field’s text (not a secret one’s). Describe says it for a control of your own, after it, in every frame:

if interfaceContext.Clickable("beacon", layout, looks, drawSwitch) {
	beacon = !beacon
}
interfaceContext.Describe(ui.Description{Role: ui.SwitchRole, Label: "Beacon", Checked: beacon})

What Describe leaves empty stays as the boxes tell it. Hidden leaves out a decoration, or a label read already with the field it names. A control with an icon alone and no label is read by the Tooltip that follows it (“Go back”), as the pointer’s note already says what it does. With no tooltip it is read by the name its label hides: IconButton("trash-2", "##Delete the note") shows the icon alone and is read «Delete the note». With neither, it is read by the icon’s name (“arrow left”) for want of a better one: describe it with what it does. A drag source is read by its name, as what it holds are controls of their own. After a menu button or a select, Describe describes the button, even while its list is open over it.

What the screen reader asks

Call What it does
SetScreenReader(on) Makes the list of nodes every frame while a screen reader is on
SetScreenReaderFocus(id) Rings the node the screen reader is on
ScreenReaderAct(id, action) Done in the next frame: a press clicks the control as a tap would (a field takes the keyboard), a long press is read by LongPressed, forward and backward scroll an area or move a slider a step
ScreenReaderSetText(id, text) Text the screen reader types, pastes or dictates into a field: the field takes it whole, as typing (one line, cut at its MaxLength), and the keyboard stays down

Nodes keep their ids from frame to frame, so the screen reader stays on what it was reading. On Android, the window hands the list to Go Renderer, which gives it to TalkBack (Android).
