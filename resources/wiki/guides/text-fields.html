⌨️ Text fields

A text field edits your string as it is typed: TextField("Name", &entry.name) changes entry.name on the step a key is pressed, and returns true then.

Characters come from the system after the keyboard layout and dead keys, so accents and «ñ» type as anywhere else. Line breaks and tabs pasted in become spaces: the field has one line. Text longer than the field slides sideways to keep the caret in sight.

Focus

Focus(path…) gives the keyboard to a field by its scopes and label, as Area finds it, with its text selected: the search box on a shortcut, or the first field of a form that just opened. While a field has the focus, End claims the keyboard, so the viewer’s shortcuts and cameras do not take the letters. By the field’s name instead, FieldFocused(name) tells whether it has the keyboard and FocusField(name) gives it the keyboard.

On a phone

A phone’s on-screen keyboard edits the field’s text rather than typing key by key: it suggests words, keeps the word it is still making underlined (composing) and deletes around the caret. TypedField() tells the window which field is typed in, with its text, selection and composing word, and EditTypedField(edit) puts the keyboard’s edit in, in the next frame, as typing: one line, cut at MaxLength, its words undone as typed ones are. A key or a touch that changes the text ends the composing. The Android window does both (Android).

On a touch screen, text is chosen as on a phone. A finger held on a word selects it: a drop-shaped handle hangs under each end of the selection, and a bar over it offers Cut, Copy, Paste and Select all (SetEditingWords in the person’s language, EditingWords to read them; a secret field cannot be cut or copied). A touch shows a handle under the caret. A finger drags a handle to move its end, or the caret; the handle hangs under the text, so the finger does not hide what it chooses, and the ends never cross. The handles and the bar are popups of the field’s own: pressing them keeps the field typed in. Typing, on either keyboard, puts them away; a mouse has none.
