🖱️ Mouse, keyboard and touch

Go UI shares the mouse and the keyboard with what is behind it: a 3D view’s camera, the viewer’s shortcuts. This page says who gets them, and how fingers are followed on a touch screen.

Who gets the mouse

Controls react to the mouse using where they were laid out in the previous frame (sizes are only known at End). The pointer is a frame behind what is drawn, which nobody notices at sixty frames a second.

  • Hover: the front-most panel under the pointer is the only one whose controls can be hovered. Within it, the first control to find the pointer over it takes it; containers that react to the mouse (a title bar) look after their content, so the buttons inside them win.
  • Press and click: a pressed control keeps the mouse until the button is let go, even if the pointer strays. A click is a press and a release over the same control.
  • The scene keeps its drags: a press on empty screen belongs to the camera until it is let go; panels it passes over do not take it.
  • Claims: at End, when the pointer is over a panel or a control is pressed, the interface calls state.ClaimMouse(). Go Renderer’s viewer then gives its cameras and shortcuts the input without the mouse, so dragging inside a panel does not turn the view. A text field with the focus claims the keyboard the same way. WantsMouse() and WantsKeyboard() tell your own code.
  • Pointer shape: a hand over buttons, four arrows over a title bar that can be dragged and while dragging a payload, an I-beam over a text field, a two-headed arrow over a resize handle.
  • Modal and dragging: while a sheet is open or a payload is dragged, the interface claims the mouse and the keyboard wherever the pointer is.

Which layer is in front, and so gets the mouse first, is drawn in panels and layers.

Fingers

On a touch screen, one finger is followed from where it comes down: the way it goes first decides who takes it, the innermost scroll area for up and down, a SwipeBox for sideways (pages, cards in a row, a row swiped away), and the control it came down on is then not touched. A finger that stays still on a control for half a second long-presses it (LongPressed).

Call What it does
SwipeBox(name, layout, axis, content) Takes sideways swipes: pages, cards in a row, a row swiped away
LongPressed() Whether the control just added was long-pressed: a finger held still for half a second, or the right mouse button
ScrollPull(name) Measures a pull past a list’s top, for a list that refreshes
ScrollOffset(name) How far an area is scrolled, for a bar that shrinks over it
Context.Touches() The fingers on a touch screen, for a control of a program’s own that follows them (an on-screen stick); the first finger is also the pointer that clicks
Context.Back Answers a phone’s back gesture; modals and sheets answer it first

Scroll areas glide on when let go. How touches reach Go UI on a phone is in Android.
