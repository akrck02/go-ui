Tutorial: a register of lighthouses

In seven lessons you build a real desktop application, one step at a time: a register of lighthouses with a list to choose one, a form to edit it, a search, a menu, a sheet that asks before removing, and a control of your own. Then you put an interface over a 3D scene, and take the program to a web page and a phone.

Before you start, follow Get started. Each lesson takes ten to twenty minutes and ends with something you can run.

Every lesson has the same shape, as in a quest: the idea in everyday words, the code, a checkpoint to know it works, a bonus with its solution folded, and an achievement to unlock. A final challenge waits at the end of lesson 7.

Lesson You learn You end with
1. Immediate mode A frame, controls that return what happened, ids and scopes A lamp to switch on and off
2. Layout Boxes, directions, Fit, Grow, Fixed, padding, gaps, frames The window’s skeleton: toolbar, side list, content, status bar
3. Controls and forms Selectable rows, text and number fields, selects, sliders, toggles A form that edits the lighthouse chosen
4. Menus, sheets and notes Menus, modal sheets, toasts, the search Adding and removing lighthouses
5. Controls of your own Clickable, looks, Describe for screen readers, Paint, themes A favourite star, and a theme of your own
6. Over a 3D scene Go UI with Go Renderer’s viewer, claims, Options.Scene A panel that moves the sun over an island
7. Shipping it Preferences, leaving the screen, the web, Android The register in a browser and on a phone

Lessons end with practice, with a solution folded under it. The complete program is at the end of lesson 5.

💡 Every program here takes -capture shot.png (one frame to a picture), -dark, -width and -height, through window.Options.RegisterFlags. That is how the pictures were made.
